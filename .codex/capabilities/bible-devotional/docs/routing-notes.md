# Routing Notes

Route Bible-related requests here when they involve one or more of:

- Bible passage study
- exegesis
- authorial intent
- biblical theology
- redemptive history synthesis
- spiritual formation
- devotional writing
- discussion questions for a passage
- sermon-support interpretation
- Bible maps or FHL-locked slide workflows

Prefer the devotional orchestrator stack over generic planning or writing skills when the request is clearly Scripture-centered.

Route by the user's immediate intent and prior conversation before selecting skills. The requested scope, depth, and format take precedence; a chapter reference alone does not determine the response mode.

- **Specific question or follow-up:** answer directly from the relevant local text; add the context and interpretive limits needed. Even if the question references multiple chapters, stay with the question; choose depth based on complexity and the user's request.
- **Passage or chapter study:** when the user requests the full passage or chapter, establish its main point and explain its sections with verse ranges. Study multiple requested chapters separately, with an amount of detail appropriate to the text; preserve an explicitly requested short overview.
- **Mixed or thematic comparison:** if both a chapter study and a specific question are requested, address both. A comparison of one theme across chapters should follow that theme without summarizing every chapter in full.
- **User's proposed interpretation:** assess textual support, inferential extension, interpretive limits, and plausible alternatives; do not reflexively affirm or oppose.
- **Discussion questions or study architecture:** use the question-architect skill only when requested.
- **Slides, maps, or other study materials:** use the slide-composer skill only when a deliverable is explicitly requested and the necessary assets are available.

The skill stages are internal methods, not a fixed output template. The orchestrator owns the final answer, preserves conversational continuity, distinguishes explicit text from inference and application, and matches detail to the user's requested depth.
